## Who should you call first for asbestos testing in Fresno?

Call an independent testing company first, before you call anyone who might perform the removal. An unbiased lab result is the one document every other decision in the project depends on: whether you need a contractor at all, what scope they quote, and what your lender, buyer, or insurer will accept as proof.

This matters most in Fresno's older neighborhoods. Homes built before 1980 in the Tower District, Old Fig Garden, and the Sunnyside area commonly carry textured "popcorn" ceilings, 9x9 vinyl floor tile, and wrapped furnace ductwork, all materials that can contain asbestos depending on the manufacturer and year. You cannot tell by looking. A visual guess from a contractor who also bids the removal work is not the same as a lab result from [PLM analysis run by an accredited laboratory](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos).

## When do you need a tester instead of a remediation contractor?

You need an independent tester whenever the question is still "is it asbestos," and a remediation contractor only after that question is answered yes and you have a written scope. Mixing up that order costs money and trust.

Call an independent testing company, such as [Arch for an asbestos testing appointment](/services/asbestos-testing/), when:

- You're planning a remodel, demo, or flooring tear-out in a pre-1980 Fresno home and need to clear materials before work starts
- A home inspection flagged "suspect ACM" (asbestos-containing material) during a purchase and your option period is closing
- You're a landlord or property manager who needs documentation before a tenant turnover or capital improvement
- You want a second opinion on a contractor's own "it's probably fine" assessment

Call a licensed abatement contractor only after a lab has confirmed asbestos is present and you have a written scope of what needs to come out. The [California Contractors State License Board's consumer guide](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/) lets you confirm any abatement contractor you're considering is actually certified for asbestos work before you sign anything.

If the material is actively being disturbed, say a failing roof dumping debris into a crawl space, your first call might be a roofer or general contractor to stop the damage, with testing scheduled around it. If you're mid-renovation with permits already pulled, San Joaquin Valley air district notification rules generally expect a survey before demolition, which is a reason to get testing scheduled early, not after the dumpster arrives.

## What happens when you call for asbestos testing in Fresno?

On the call, Arch schedules a site visit and asks what's prompting the test, a remodel, a purchase, a tenant complaint, so the inspector collects the right number of samples from the right materials. Have the property's rough age, the areas you're concerned about, and any upcoming deadline (close of escrow, permit date) ready to share.

On site, the inspector collects small, controlled samples, think a dime-sized piece of ceiling texture or a tile corner, wets the material first to limit fiber release, and seals each sample in a labeled bag with a chain-of-custody form. Nothing is torn out or disturbed beyond the sample itself. Samples go to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy, the standard method for identifying asbestos fibers in bulk material.

The written report lists each sample location, the material type, and the lab's finding. If a material comes back positive, that report becomes the scope document for whichever licensed abatement contractor you hire; it is never an upsell, because Arch does not perform removal.

## Fresno's housing stock is why this question comes up so often

Fresno's building boom ran heaviest from the 1950s through the 1970s, so a large share of the city's housing stock predates the 1980s shift away from asbestos-containing building materials. Popcorn ceilings, mastic under old vinyl tile, and wrap on original ductwork are common finds in these homes, not because something went wrong, but because they were standard materials for the era.

None of that means a home has a problem. It means guessing is the wrong strategy.
