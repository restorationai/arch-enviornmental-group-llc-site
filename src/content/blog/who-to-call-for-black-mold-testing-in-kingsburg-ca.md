## Who do you call first: an independent tester, a home inspector, or a remediation company?

Call an independent environmental testing company first, not a remediation or mold-removal company, when you want an unbiased answer about whether mold is actually present. A company that profits from remediation work has a built-in reason to find a problem large enough to justify a contract. A company that only tests has nothing riding on the outcome either way.

A general home inspector is a useful first flag, not a final answer. Most home inspectors note visible staining or a musty smell as a condition to investigate further, but they are not trained to collect air or surface samples or identify what's growing. California does not issue a specific state license for mold inspectors the way it licenses contractors through the CSLB. That gap means it's worth asking any inspector what training they have and what sampling protocol they follow, rather than assuming a title guarantees a method.

This is also where the handoff matters most: once a lab confirms mold, the testing company should step back and let you hire a separate, qualified remediation contractor for the actual cleanup. If the removal work ever needs to be verified, that confirmation should come from the same kind of independent source that found it in the first place, not from the contractor checking its own work. [Why your mold tester should never be your mold remover](/blog/why-independent-mold-testing-matters/) goes into why that separation exists in this industry.

## What does a black mold test actually include?

A standard inspection combines a visual walkthrough, moisture readings at suspect areas, and air or surface samples sent to an accredited third-party lab for identification. The inspector isn't just looking at the stain; they're measuring the moisture behind it and comparing what's in your air to an outdoor baseline sample.

Typical steps: a moisture meter reading at the suspect wall or ceiling, an air sample pulled through a spore trap cassette in the affected room and again outdoors for comparison, and sometimes a tape lift or swab directly off the discolored surface. Each sample gets logged, sealed, and shipped under chain of custody to a lab, which returns a report identifying spore types and concentrations. Costs vary by home size and how many samples are collected; [the typical cost breakdown for Kingsburg](/blog/black-mold-testing-cost-in-kingsburg-ca/) walks through the ranges. ## Is it black mold, or just mildew, and is testing worth it?

Color and smell alone can't confirm whether something is mold, mildew, or a mineral stain, which is exactly why lab analysis exists. Many Central Valley homes develop dark surface staining from condensation, hard water, or old roof patches that has nothing to do with active mold growth.

Testing is worth the cost when there's a documented history of moisture (a past leak, a flood, a known plumbing failure), when a musty smell persists after visible cleaning, or when a transaction deadline requires written documentation rather than a visual opinion. It's less necessary for a small, clearly dry, cosmetic stain with no odor and no moisture source. The EPA notes that [mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) can begin growing on a damp surface within one to two days, and the CDC's guidance on [mold and health](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html) explains why addressing the moisture source matters as much as identifying the mold itself.

## What happens when you call, and what should you do while you wait?

When you call, have the basics ready: where you see staining or smell odor, whether there's a known moisture source, and any history of leaks or water intrusion. That lets the inspector plan which rooms to sample and whether air, surface, or both types of testing make sense for your situation.

On arrival, expect a visual walkthrough, moisture readings, and sample collection, typically followed by a lab turnaround of a few business days for results. While you wait: don't disturb the suspect area or try to clean it yourself, since that can scatter spores and skew a later retest. Do address any active moisture source right away, calling a plumber or roofer if water is still getting in. Running a dehumidifier in the room is reasonable; starting demolition or remediation before you have results is not.

If the report does confirm mold, the next call is to a qualified, independent remediation contractor for the removal itself. Once that work is done, [schedule a call](/contact/) for independent clearance testing to confirm the space is actually clear before you close it back up.
