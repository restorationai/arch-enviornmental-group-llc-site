## Who do you actually need to call about mold in your Kingsburg home?

It depends on where you are in the process. If mold hasn't been removed yet, you need a licensed remediation or abatement contractor to scope the work and do the physical removal. Once that work is finished, you need an independent tester, not the same contractor, to verify the space is actually clear before you move back in, close out an insurance claim, or hand keys to a buyer.

There's a third call too: if the moisture source is still active, a leaking roof, a slow supply line, a crawl space that floods every wet season, a plumber or roofer needs to fix that before any clearance test means anything. Testing a still-wet space just produces a result that won't hold. Many of the older ranch homes and farmhouses around Kingsburg and nearby Selma and Reedley have original crawl spaces that hold moisture longer than newer slab construction, so confirming the source is dry is step one, not an afterthought.

## What happens when you call Arch Environmental Group for clearance testing?

The first call is short: we ask for the remediation scope of work, the address, and whether containment (plastic sheeting sealing off the work area) is still up or already removed. Have your original mold inspection report handy if you have one; it helps us compare before-and-after conditions in the same rooms.

On site, the inspector does a visual check for leftover dust, debris, or staining, checks moisture content with a meter, and then runs indoor air samples with a spore trap cassette, a small device an air pump pulls a measured volume of air through, in each remediated area. We also pull at least one outdoor baseline sample, because a clearance result only means something compared against the air outside your own house. Samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory, and typical lab turnaround in this industry runs 24 to 72 hours depending on the analysis requested. You can see the full process and typical pricing on our [clearance testing page](/services/clearance-testing/), or get the local cost breakdown in [Mold Clearance Testing Cost in Kingsburg, CA](/blog/mold-clearance-testing-cost-in-kingsburg-ca/).

## What happens if a mold clearance test fails?

A failed clearance test means the report shows indoor spore counts or conditions still above the outdoor baseline, and it goes back to the remediation contractor with specifics on where and what. It is not a vague "try again."

The contractor re-cleans the flagged area, and then you schedule another round of independent testing before containment comes down for good. According to [EPA guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), visible mold and the conditions that caused it need to be fully addressed, not just masked, before a space is considered resolved. A professional remediation job typically follows the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/) for mold remediation, which is part of why post-work verification is a standard step, not an upsell.

If you're staring at a closing deadline, an insurance deadline, or a family member with respiratory sensitivities waiting to move back in, the fastest path is still the right order: confirm the moisture source is fixed, confirm remediation is complete, then [schedule independent clearance testing](tel:+15592962088) rather than skipping straight back into the space.
