A simple pre-remodel screening on a single-family home usually lands near the lower end; a rental turnover inspection with dust clearance testing or a combined lead-and-asbestos visit runs higher. If you own a Craftsman bungalow near the Tower District or a 1950s ranch in one of Fresno's older postwar tracts, there's a good chance your home was painted before 1978, the year the federal government banned lead-based residential paint. Maybe your contractor flagged peeling trim before a kitchen remodel, or you're a landlord preparing a unit for new tenants and want to document the paint condition before someone asks questions. Either way, the first real question is cost, and the honest answer depends on which kind of lead test you actually need.

## How much does lead paint testing cost in Fresno, CA?

Lead paint testing in Fresno typically runs $300 to $900, with most single-family screenings falling between $300 and $600 and full risk assessments running closer to $900. The spread comes down to testing method, how many components get checked, and whether dust or soil samples are added to the scope.

| Scenario | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| XRF lead paint screening, single-family home (interior and exterior components) | $300 - $600 |
| Full lead risk assessment (XRF plus dust wipes and soil samples) | $450 - $900 |
| Paint chip lab confirmation samples, per sample (AAS/ICP analysis) | $40 - $75 each |
| Lead testing combined with an asbestos survey in the same visit | $600 - $1,200 |
| Rental unit turnover clearance dust wipe testing | $200 - $400 |

These are typical industry ranges, not a quote. A property's age, number of painted components, and accessibility (a two-story Victorian near Fig Garden has more trim, sills, and doors to check than a single-story ranch) all move the number. A written scope should always come before the inspector shows up with equipment.

## XRF vs paint chip lab samples: what's the price difference and why?

An XRF (X-ray fluorescence) reading costs less per test because it gives an instant, non-destructive result on site, while a paint chip sent to an accredited lab costs more per sample but confirms a result the XRF can't resolve. Most jobs use XRF as the primary method and reserve lab samples for edge cases.

The XRF analyzer is a handheld device the inspector presses against a painted surface, a window casing, a baseboard, a door jamb, and reads a lead concentration in milligrams per square centimeter within seconds. No surface damage, no lab turnaround, and it can check dozens of components in one visit. That's why it's the default for most Fresno lead inspections.

Paint chip sampling becomes necessary when the XRF reading falls in an inconclusive range, or when a lab-confirmed result is needed for legal or transaction documentation. The inspector carefully removes a small paint chip down to the substrate, bags and labels it with chain of custody paperwork, and sends it to a third-party lab for analysis by atomic absorption or ICP spectroscopy. That precision costs more per sample, which is why a job needing several lab confirmations lands at the higher end of the range.

## Lead inspection vs lead risk assessment: why does the price differ?

A lead inspection tells you where lead-based paint exists in a home; a lead risk assessment tells you whether that paint is creating an actual hazard right now, and it costs more because it covers more ground. Knowing which one you need keeps you from paying for scope you don't want.

An inspection surveys painted components throughout the property and reports lead content component by component. It's the right call before a remodel, when a contractor needs to know what they're disturbing before cutting into a wall or sanding trim.

A risk assessment goes further: it adds dust wipe sampling from floors and windowsills, sometimes soil sampling near the foundation, and a visual condition survey of deteriorated paint, then weighs all of it against the EPA's hazard standards. That's the format landlords and property managers often need for a rental turnover, since it documents not just presence but exposure risk. The [EPA's Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Program](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program) outlines when contractors working in pre-1978 housing are required to follow work practices, and a risk assessment report is often the document that clarifies which rooms those rules apply to.

## Do I need lead testing before a remodel in a pre-1978 Fresno home?

Yes, if your home was built before 1978 and the renovation will disturb painted surfaces, testing before the work starts is worth the cost because federal rules otherwise require the contractor to presume lead is present. That presumption triggers containment and cleanup requirements even if the paint turns out to be lead-free.

Much of central Fresno's housing stock, the older bungalows and postwar tracts closer to downtown and the Tower District, predates 1978 by decades. A confirmed negative result from an XRF survey can let a remodel proceed under standard work practices instead of full containment, which often saves more on the project than the test itself cost. A confirmed positive result gives the contractor exact locations to manage, instead of treating the whole house as suspect.

## Should lead and asbestos testing happen together?

Often yes, because homes old enough to carry lead-based paint are frequently old enough to carry asbestos-containing materials too, and combining both into one visit usually costs less than two separate appointments. Vermiculite attic insulation, textured "popcorn" ceilings, and vinyl floor tile mastic from the same era are common asbestos suspects in the same pre-1978 Fresno homes where lead paint shows up.

If a remodel is going to disturb flooring, ceiling texture, or siding in addition to painted trim, it's worth scoping both tests in the same visit. ## Who pays for lead paint testing?

In a home sale, the buyer typically orders and pays for lead testing during the inspection contingency period, then uses the results to negotiate repairs or credits with the seller. In a rental, California law generally places housing obligations on the landlord, so the property owner usually pays for pre-turnover testing. For a remodel, the homeowner or the general contractor typically covers the cost as part of pre-construction due diligence, since it determines which work practices the project must follow.

## What happens during a lead paint testing visit?

1. **Scope call.** The inspector confirms the property's age, the number of painted components, and whether you need an inspection only or a full risk assessment.
2. **On-site XRF survey.** Each painted component, windows, doors, trim, siding, is read and logged against its location.
3. **Lab samples if needed.** Any inconclusive readings get a paint chip pulled, bagged, and sent out under chain of custody.
4. **Dust or soil sampling, if scoped.** For a risk assessment, wipe samples are collected from floors and sills and sent to an accredited lab.
5. **Written report.** Results come back component by component with lead concentrations, so you and your contractor know exactly what's in play, and what isn't.
