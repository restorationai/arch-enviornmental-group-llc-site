## What certifications should an asbestos testing company in Fresno hold?

A qualified asbestos inspector in California holds a Certified Asbestos Consultant (CAC) credential issued through Cal/OSHA DOSH, the state agency that regulates asbestos-related work. This is not optional paperwork; it's the license that lets someone legally identify suspect materials, collect samples, and write a report state and local agencies will accept.

Arch Environmental Group's inspectors carry the Certified Asbestos Consultant credential, along with Lead Inspector/Assessor certification and NIOSH 582 microscopy training, the federal course that qualifies an analyst to read asbestos fiber samples under a microscope. Ask any company you're considering which specific credential their inspector holds and whether you can see the certificate. A vague "yes, we're certified" with no name or number is a red flag.

## What should a complete asbestos inspection in Fresno include?

A complete inspection includes a visual walkthrough of suspect materials, physical bulk samples from each distinct material type, a documented chain of custody, and a written report with lab results you can hand to a contractor or submit for a permit. Anything less leaves you guessing.

In practice that means the inspector identifies materials likely to contain asbestos (popcorn ceiling texture, vinyl floor tile and its mastic, certain stucco and plaster systems, HVAC duct wrap, and older roofing or siding products common in Fresno's mid-century housing stock), collects a small sample of each with the area lightly misted to control fiber release, bags and labels every sample, and sends the batch to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis, with transmission electron microscopy (TEM) available for lower-detection-limit cases. The written report should state material type, location, lab result, and percentage by weight, in a format usable if your project needs a demolition or renovation notification through the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District. If you're planning a renovation in particular, see [why an asbestos test before renovation matters](/blog/asbestos-test-before-renovation/) for what skipping this step can cost later.

The [EPA's asbestos program](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos) outlines the health basis for why pre-renovation testing matters in buildings constructed before the 1980s, when many of these materials were still in common use.

## Should the same company test and remove asbestos?

No. An asbestos testing company should never also perform the removal, because that arrangement gives the tester a financial incentive to find a problem worth billing for. Keeping testing and abatement separate is the single biggest thing to check before you hire anyone.

Arch Environmental Group performs testing only. It has no abatement division and no referral fee riding on your result, which means a negative result costs the company nothing and a positive result doesn't fund a second contract. If your sample does come back positive, the next step is hiring a qualified, independent asbestos abatement contractor to do the removal, then bringing in an independent tester again for clearance air sampling once the work is done. Clearance testing is how you verify the contractor's own work, which is exactly why it shouldn't come from the contractor. Our [clearance testing](/services/asbestos-testing/) is a separate scope from the original inspection for this reason.

## What questions should you ask before hiring a Fresno asbestos inspector?

Ask these four questions before you book anyone, and expect a direct answer to each: Which Cal/OSHA DOSH certification do you hold, and can I see it? Which laboratory analyzes your samples, and is it independently accredited? Do you also perform abatement, or only testing? Will the written report include lab results I can hand to a contractor or submit to the air district?

A company that hesitates on any of these, especially the second and third, is worth passing on. The whole point of hiring an independent tester is getting a report nobody has a stake in.
