## What should I look for when choosing a lead paint testing company in Kingsburg?

Look for three things before you book: a verifiable state lead certification, a clear answer on testing method (XRF or lab sample), and independence from the remediation side of the work. Everything else, like how fast someone answers the phone, is secondary to these.

California regulates who is legally allowed to perform lead paint inspections for pre-1978 housing. A company that can't tell you, in one sentence, who on their team holds that certification and what it covers shouldn't be your first call. Ask for the certification type by name, not just "we're certified."

## Is the inspector CDPH-certified as a Lead Inspector/Assessor?

Yes, that's the specific California credential to ask for, and you can check it yourself before you ever sign anything. California's lead-related construction program, run through the [California Department of Public Health](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/CLPPB/Pages/find_lead_prof.aspx), maintains a public directory of certified lead professionals, including the Lead Inspector/Assessor category that covers pre-sale and pre-renovation testing.

Arch Enviornmental Group LLC's team holds certification as a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector/Assessor, along with a NIOSH 582 equivalent microscopist credential used for asbestos fiber analysis. ## XRF vs lab paint-chip samples: which method should the company use?

A portable XRF analyzer reads lead content through paint layers in seconds and is the most common method for whole-house inspections; lab analysis of a small paint chip sample is slower but useful for confirming borderline readings or multi-layer paint systems. Most Kingsburg inspections combine both as needed rather than relying on just one.

An X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analyzer is pressed directly against a painted surface, like a window casing or a baseboard, and gives a reading on the spot. It's non-destructive and fast, which is why it's the standard tool for room-by-room testing. The tradeoff: readings near the regulatory threshold sometimes need a confirming paint chip, which is cut, bagged, and sent to an accredited lab.

A company worth hiring should explain which method they're using and why, not just show up with one tool and call it comprehensive. If your property has a lot of painted trim original to its construction era, ask whether the inspector plans a representative sampling strategy across rooms, or just a handful of spot checks.

## Does the company also perform lead abatement, and why does that matter?

It matters because a company that profits from finding lead has a built-in incentive to find it, and a company that only tests has no such incentive. This is the independence question, and it's worth asking directly: "If you find lead, who does the removal work?"

Arch Enviornmental Group LLC tests. It does not perform abatement, demolition, or paint removal. That separation means the report you get back reflects what the XRF readings and any lab results actually show, not what makes the next invoice bigger. If results call for abatement, that report hands off cleanly to any qualified, licensed abatement contractor you choose, it isn't written to steer you toward a specific crew.

The same logic applies after abatement work is done: clearance testing that confirms the job is actually finished should come from someone who didn't do the removal. That's not a knock on remediation contractors, it's just how an unbiased result works. For more on why this separation exists across environmental testing generally, see [why your mold tester shouldn't be your mold remover](/blog/why-independent-mold-testing-matters/), which covers the same principle.

## What should the written report include, and how fast should I get it?

A usable lead paint testing report lists every component tested (room by room), the result for each (positive, negative, or inconclusive), the method used, and a summary a lender, buyer, or contractor can act on without calling to ask questions. Typical lab turnaround across the industry runs 24 to 72 hours when a paint chip sample is involved; XRF-only results are often available the same day since there's no outside lab step.

Ask to see a sample report, with addresses redacted, before you book. If the company can't produce one, that's worth noticing. A report that just says "lead detected" or "lead not detected" with no component breakdown isn't going to hold up for a real estate transaction or satisfy a renovation permit requirement.

Lead paint itself isn't new information. The federal government banned residential lead-based paint sales in 1978, which is why anything built before that date gets flagged for testing in the first place. The [EPA's lead program](https://www.epa.gov/lead) covers the broader regulatory background if you want it, but for a specific property, the only thing that actually answers the question is a certified inspection done on-site.
